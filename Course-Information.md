## 2. Schedule and Class Format

- **Credits (Hours):** 3 (4)
- **Intended For:** Second-year students (major required)
- **Language of Instruction:** English
- **Class Times:** Monday 10:00 ~ 10:50, 11:00 ~ 11:50, Wednesday 09:00 ~ 09:50, 10:00 ~ 10:50
- **Classroom:** Room 7-310 (Mon, Wed)
- **Class Format:** Lectures. Classes are primarily held in person, and they may be replaced with online sessions depending on COVID-19 policies.
- **Course Description:** The course covers the fundamental concepts of computer science, including logic, proofs, functions, relations, and graphs, and building on these fundamentals it also addresses algebraic structures, Boolean algebra, and algorithms. Along with data structures, it is one of the most essential introductory courses in computer science and provides the groundwork for understanding the upper-level courses of the third and fourth years.
- **Lectures:** Lectures are based on the textbook. Students are encouraged to review the relevant reading materials in advance according to the course schedule.
- **Practice:** Students are expected to solve the problems presented in the assignments on their own.
- **Prerequisites:** There is no prerequisite course.
- **Attendance:** Attendance is checked.
- **Study Objectives:** Students who complete the course are expected to develop the following.
  1. A solid understanding of discrete mathematics concepts
  2. Analytical and problem-solving skills
  3. The ability to apply discrete mathematics knowledge to various areas of computer science

## 4. Course Policies

- The submission deadline is strict, and all late assignments are graded zero. No exceptions to the homework policy are made without prior instructor approval, and medical exceptions require proper documentation.
- Students are encouraged to discuss class topics among themselves, but each student must complete the assignments independently. Any sharing of code or plagiarism is considered academic dishonesty and results in strict disciplinary action.
- Regular attendance is expected.
- In all processes of taking the course (attending lectures, writing and submitting assignments, exams, etc.), students must participate in learning with honesty and passion according to the purpose and educational philosophy of the university, devote themselves to creative learning based on moral sensitivity, faithfully carry out what they have promised the faculty and fellow students, and respect the character of the faculty and other students and observe basic manners.
- Credits obtained through dishonest means such as plagiarism, reuse of assignments from other classes, or cheating on exams are revoked even after they have been granted, and disciplinary action such as suspension, withdrawal, or expulsion may follow depending on the circumstances.
- The university operates a plagiarism inspection system (Blackboard) to raise awareness of learning ethics and to encourage creativity, and students must check in advance whether their submissions constitute plagiarism.
- Students with questions about support services for students with disabilities can consult Prof. Shinhoo Kang to find the best way to take the course for their type of disability.
